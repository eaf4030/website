the University of Texas at Austin.

My research is in mechanism design, auctions, and the economics of
marketplaces. Lately I've been focused on what happens when AI agents interact
with one another.

<div class="paper">
  <h3>Do LLM Agents Respond to Commitment? A Behavioral Experiment in Strategic Communication</h3>
  <p class="venue">Working paper, November 2026</p>
  <p>
    As AI agents increasingly make decisions and communicate on behalf of
    individuals and organizations, understanding how they respond to
    institutional constraints becomes important. Commitment can make strategic
    communication informative by restricting how a sender may revise its
    communication after learning private information. We study whether LLM
    agents respond to mechanically enforced commitment as economic theory
    predicts, using the sender-receiver environment of Fréchette, Lizzeri, and
    Perego (2022). The experiment varies commitment strength and message
    verifiability, allowing comparisons with theoretical predictions and
    existing human experimental evidence. We find substantial differences
    across model configurations. Claude Sonnet 4.5 exhibits the
    commitment-blindness pattern documented among human subjects, whereas
    Claude Opus 5.5 and Gemini 3.8 Flash behave much closer to theory under
    full commitment. Under partial commitment, even models that make
    individually sensible revision decisions may choose initial communication
    plans that fail to anticipate those decisions. Our findings highlight the
    importance of evaluating strategic AI agents under different institutional
    arrangements rather than inferring their behavior from performance in a
    single environment.
  </p>
